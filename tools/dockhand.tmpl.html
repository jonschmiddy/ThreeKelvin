what the two generations taught

The repo's workflow doc says never to use create_character — but that warning is about ships, which are not characters. For people it is exactly right: a skeleton-rigged humanoid walk pipeline, and it cost one generation, not the 20–40 that create_image_pro would have.

Standard mode returns a bigger canvas than you ask for. I asked for 80px and got a 112×112 sheet with an 85px person in it. The room places a walker by its feet, so 27px of unmeasured padding is how a figure ends up hovering — it is cropped to its own ink before anything uses it.

Naming parts worked again. The jacket, the tabard, the satchel strap and the boots all arrived; nothing had to be re-rolled.

A walk cycle carries its own speed, and it cannot be guessed. How far a sprite strides is a fact about the art — not about the frame count, the figure's height or the template's name. Move the body further than the legs say and the planted foot skates; move it less and the character moonwalks. It is measured from the pixels now (tools/walk_stride.py) and carried beside the sprite, so no future walker can be given a number somebody picked.

The six-frame template was rolled first and cut. It strides 37.5px for an 85px person — 0.44 of its own height, against a real walk's 0.75 — so even with the arithmetic right it read as quick little steps. The eight-frame one covers 0.87 and walks. One generation each; the loser cost one and settled the question.
