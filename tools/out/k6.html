The stubborn seven, steered

Every take sits beside the card as the game draws it today — the orange tile heading each row, at the same zoom as the candidates, so the comparison is against the thing being replaced rather than against the other options.

Your seven directions, one round. 69 of 76 drawn; these are the rest.

Two of your picks change what a card in this game is allowed to be

Bore Sight (B) and Range (A) both put the viewer behind the instrument rather than looking at it. Every one of the 69 cards that landed is a thing seen from outside; these two are a point of view. If they work, that is a new frame the set did not have — and it is worth knowing whether it sits alongside the others in a hand or fights them, which is a judgement the tile alone will not settle. Look at them on the card.

What the other five were asked to do

Fire Solution (A) stops drawing arithmetic and draws a decision as a mechanical fact: a brass dial rolled hard against its stop pin, the pin taking the load. Foreknowledge (B) stops solving how the shard is mounted and makes it old — plating deformed around it over years, welds crowding its faces at unplanned angles, everything patched except the block. Reroute (C) shows the consequence: half the board dead, half alive, one cord across the line. Set Feet (B) is fully seated — locking dogs all turned home, no springs anywhere, which keeps it clear of Dig In. Feed (B) is the moment of seating: a cartridge home in an open breech, the block sliding shut behind it.

All 28 clean on the white check. If a card still misses here, the useful note is not “cut” but whether the direction was wrong or only the execution — that is the difference between picking another letter and firing the same idea again.
