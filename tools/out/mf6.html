Round 5 split the Ghost problem cleanly in half

The four takes off the Range Card reference were a thin bezel around a big black rectangle — a window onto stars, again. The four off Slag were proper dense consoles with a scan-lined CRT set into them. So the composition is solved, and the failure moved inside the screen: what sat on the glass was a formless white blob every time.

The words doing that are mine — smear, bloom, echo — every one of them a shape with no edges. Replaced with a count and a size: two small bright dots, one fainter, a short straight tail behind each, three other dots single and sharp. Both prompts use the Slag reference now. This is attempt four; if the trace still doesn't read, the honest move is a different subject rather than a fifth screen.

Standing Load was four takes of generic girderwork with nothing resting on it — “taking the weight of a far larger structure” described a relationship the frame never showed. The larger thing is now in the picture: the frame is wedged under its corner.

The white check found a bar it would have missed yesterday

Standing Load take 2 last round had a white column 57 pixels of 60 tall straight down the middle at 21% white overall — under the 25% whole-image threshold, and nowhere near an edge, so both halves of my check waved it through while it was obvious to the eye. The check only scanned the outer five columns, because that is where the previous bar had been. A check written around the last failure only catches the last failure. It now walks every column and every row, and it flagged three takes in this round: standing_load 1 and 2, keelbound 1 — judge them on the picture, but that is what the marks mean.

I also tried to automate the stray-pixel check after the Slag pixel you caught. It does not work and I have dropped it: at the loosest useful threshold it fires 63 times across the 43 shipped cards, and every one of those is correct art — stars, weld sparks, rivet specular. A tighter variant missed the Slag pixel outright. What made that pixel wrong was not isolation but that it was the brightest colour in the picture sitting in a dark corner of machinery, which is a judgment about what the picture is of.
