Four notes, one mistake, and it is a rule of mine overshot

“Bore sight should look like a sight”, “range should look like a scope or an optic”, “a vice grip doesn’t make sense”, “it looks like a wall socket instead of an A/V plug”. That is the same correction four times.

The project has a hard-won rule — name the parts, never the category — because saying “gun” gets a handgun every time. I over-applied it into describing mechanisms instead of objects: “a servo stopped on its mark”, “a drum in its detent”. A mechanism has no outside, so the model drew a housing — four featureless boxes with cables on them, twice.

The correction is that the card’s own name is a part, not a category. Bore Sight is a sight, so say sight, with a reticle in the glass. Range is an optic. Patch is a bolted sleeve with frozen sealant, not a workshop clamp. Reroute is a jack patchbay with cords in it — and it now reads as one.

Two things the checks caught, honestly reported

The banned-word audit fired on my own prompt. Reroute said “its slim barrel plug”, and barrel is on the list that exists because it produces side-on hero guns. It did no harm here — there is enough patchbay around it — but I ran the audit after spending the generations rather than before, which is the wrong order and my mistake.

Cold Sights and Range both flag heavily for white — Cold Sights at 33–50%, Range at 32–36%. Look at those eight carefully. Frost and a bright lens are exactly the two subjects where the check cannot tell a real background from the picture, so it may be either.
