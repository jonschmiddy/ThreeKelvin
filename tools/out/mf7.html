One of these has a figure in it — read that before you judge it

Ghost 3 put a tall thin human silhouette on the glass. It is the creepiest of the four by a distance, and it is also the one thing the malfunction brief explicitly rules out: “No figures.” That rule was written to keep crew out of pictures of broken machinery, and a person-shaped return on a sensor screen is arguably the opposite — the point is that there is nobody there. But it is your call and not mine, so I am flagging it rather than quietly taking it.

The white background, and the check that missed it four times

You were right that all four Standing Loads had one. Mine flagged two. The takes measured 14–21% white, with the white broken up between the girders so no single column or row was two-thirds anything, and my threshold sat at 40%.

The test that works is not a percentage — it is connectedness. A background is white that reaches the frame edge and joins up; a highlight is white that does not. Flood-filled from the border, those four measure 12, 16, 16 and 19 per cent, and 39 of the 43 shipped cards measure zero. The margin is real but not wide: Coolloss is a legitimate 11%, because its steam plume genuinely runs off the edge. So the line is 12, and it is a prompt to look rather than a verdict.

The prompt fix was the same lesson from a different angle. card_batch already records that a prompt must always say what fills the rest of the frame — I had been applying that to the space around a subject and never to the gaps inside one. These three now say what sits behind the girders. Standing Load and Keelbound came back clean on all eight; cold_store 4 still flags.
