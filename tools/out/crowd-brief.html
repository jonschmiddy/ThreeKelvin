Crowd Brief

Hundreds of people on the concourse. The credits are not the problem — 9,016 left, refilling 17 Oct. The problem is that a walker currently costs five generations and eight judgement calls, and three hundred of those is a month of afternoons. Three decisions below; reply with letters.

Template animations cost 1 generation, custom prompts cost 4. There are about eighteen walk templates and only two were ever tested — walking-6-frames was cut for a short stride, walking-8-frames narrowly lost to a custom prompt. The other sixteen are unmeasured, and each carries its own stride.

1 · The walk engine what animates every character

A Survey the templates once, then reuse the winnerrecommended Eighteen generations on one existing dock hand, measuring stride and loop seam on each. The winner becomes the house walk and every character after costs 2 instead of 5. If none is good enough, I have spent 18 and we fall back to B knowing why.
B Custom prompt per character What we did today. Best quality measured so far, and it lets a slug slither and a drone hover — no template can do those. 4 generations each, and the lap length is a surprise every time.
C Templates for the humans, custom for the weird ones Almost certainly where this ends up. Picking it now just means running the survey and not pretending a template will make a slug move.

2 · Twelve to sixteen frames you asked for it; here is what it costs

Frames are driven by distance, not time, so the frame rate is pace × frames ÷ stride. Above about five a second the eye fuses the errors and that is what jitter is — you caught it before any measurement did. At today's 30 px/s the ceiling is 8–10 frames. Sixteen is not a generation problem; it is a speed problem.

3 · Where the variety comes from 100s of people, not 100s of generations

At 40 px, behind glass, in a dim room, a clown and a dockhand are the same six pixels of colour. The repo already argues the alternative — "a palette job, not a generation job: the ship is already the one that was chosen". One body in four palettes reads as four people, for nothing.

A Generate every character at every size 150 people × 3 rungs. Honest, and most of it lands where nobody can see it.
B Spend the variety where it readsrecommended 80px gets the whole cast. 60px gets a subset. 40px gets six generic bodies. Then multiply every rung by palette swaps.
C One rung only — 80px Put walk lines only where an 80px person fits and drop the far rungs. Fewest moving parts; costs the depth you built the lines for.

Two things I would fix first

None of the walker work is committed. Every tool, every strip, the whole findings section of the workflow doc — 75 files, all untracked. A campaign this size should not start on top of that.

The handbook still forbids this. docs/handbook.md and ART_CONTRACT.md both say never to use create_character, with no people exception — only the uncommitted workflow doc knows better. Any future session reading the handbook first will refuse to do this work.
