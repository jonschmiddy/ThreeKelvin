Round 4 - Hairline, Ghost, Torn Liner re-fired; brace opens

Every take sits beside the card as the game draws it today — the orange tile heading each row, at the same zoom as the candidates, so the comparison is against the thing being replaced rather than against the other options.

Five landed last round out of 36 — 14%, the best rate this project has measured, against 9% early and 1.8% at the worst of it. Ballast, Jammed Feed, Scored Barrel, Seized Actuator and Slow Burn are installed. This round is the three families that went zero, plus the first four brace cards.

What went wrong in the three you cut, read off the pictures

Hairline — the frost ate the card. I wrote “hoarfrost bleeding out of it in a feathered plume” and got, eight times out of eight, a white fern or a lightning bolt doing all the visual work while the crack itself was invisible. The frost is gone entirely. The fracture is now the subject: buckled lips, bright raw steel in the gap, corrosion around it.

Torn Liner — “ablative” is a word, not a thing. All four came back with checkerboard dither in the dark areas, which is what the model does when it has nothing to draw. Replaced with heat shielding, which it has seen, and the prompt now asks for solid dark plating rather than leaving those regions to chance.

Ghost Signal — too far back. Four handsome consoles with a smudge on one dial. The malfunction was a detail inside a wide shot. The scope face now fills the frame and the doubled echo with its smeared trail is the subject.

The four brace cards test a specific finding. One of 28 brace takes landed when the reference was Auspice or Sympathetic Burst — both single objects in empty space — and eight of those 28 came back letterboxed on white. A sparse reference makes sparse pictures however the prompt reads. These four run off Slag, which is dense. If the rule holds they should fill their frames.

Measured across all 32: no letterboxing, no edge bars, nothing above 25% white. Second clean round in a row.
