Ship's kit round 5 - nine cards, one per note

Every take sits beside the card as the game draws it today — the orange tile heading each row, at the same zoom as the candidates, so the comparison is against the thing being replaced rather than against the other options.

Four installed — 66 of 76. Chain Feed, Cold Sights, Deep Song and Overwatch. Nine cards re-fired here, one per note.

Two of these were me over-correcting your last note

Foreknowledge. You said the shard was caged like a trophy, so I integrated it — and dissolved it. Now it is flush with the structure and unmistakable in silhouette: matte black, hard-edged, geometric against grey machinery, plating meeting it along the bottom and conduits running into two faces.

Whale Song. You said the yellow was wrong, so I killed the amber — and it became cable. It is now swollen wet tissue, lumpy and irregular, veins standing up under a membrane, explicitly never tubular. The lesson both times: a note about one attribute is not permission to remove the thing that attribute was attached to.

Set Feet: you were questioning the brief, and you were right to

“Why are they spring?” — because the brief I wrote and you approved says “a spring pack under compression”. That is a mechanism, not a picture of setting your feet, and it also has nothing to do with the card's Block 12 / Feedback 3. Springs are gone entirely: two massive steel jaws driven together, teeth fully meshed, nothing moving.

Fire Solution is the same failure and your “I don't know what it's drawing” is the fair verdict. “Linked arms over a scribed plate” is a mechanism doing arithmetic, and arithmetic has no picture. It is now one line and one point, with the arm resting on the point — the answer, not the working.

Bore Sight had its shutter shut, so it is drawn back and the lens is looking out. Feed's cartridges pointed every which way; the whole rack now stands nose-up. Overside gets the half-open hatch off take 4's geometry. Reroute pushes one more step.

Range had no note but has now gone 0 for 12, so it changes shape rather than wording: a stereoscopic rangefinder — one long body with a lens window at each far end — which is a silhouette no other card in the set can be confused with.
