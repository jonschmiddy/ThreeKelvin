Twenty-Eight Cards: everything the ship does that is not a shot and not a fault

The last set, and the hardest one to keep apart. The sixteen malfunctions were sixteen different objects before I wrote a word — a crack, a plume, a seized ram. These twenty-eight are twenty-eight different verbs: vent, lock on, draw, brace, repair, block. Seven of them are Vent, six are Block or Feedback, five are Lock On. Nothing in the names stops Bleed Heat, Emergency Vent, Shunt and Absolute from all being a jet of gas leaving a pipe.

So every brief is anchored to its MODULE, not to its keyword

The card's granting part is the one thing that already differs card to card, and the game has written a line of flavour for each: Cold Sights “cools the barrel and the arithmetic”, the Ejector Rail is “a rail, a hatch, and the dark”, the Voidwhale Ganglion is “still faintly warm, the smell is overpowering”. Two cards off the same module get the same hardware and a different moment of it — Shunt is the coupling carrying, Cold Sights is the optic, Bleed Heat is the radiator behind it.

Where two cards genuinely share both module and verb, one takes the quiet reading and one the loud one. Hold Fast is struts holding; Truss is struts already bent from holding.

Three things this set must NOT inherit from the malfunctions

These are cards you choose. A malfunction is punishment and looks it — cold, still, already broken. These are equipment working, and should feel capable: something is being done on purpose and it is holding.

No lettering, anywhere. Asking for stencils is what put three lines of garbled text on Hairline, across 191 takes of every malfunction brief. Every avoid below says so.

Say what fills the frame, including the gaps. All four Standing Load takes came back with a white background showing between the girders. Every world below names what sits behind the subject and inside its gaps, not just around it.

Two colours are simply unavailable and no brief may ask for them: there is no green and no bright blue in the palette — 29 colours, every cool one a blue-grey. Ghost Signal cost twelve takes to that, and Hard Line four. Where a card wants a signal colour it gets amber, which the palette has six of.

How to use this

Approve and I generate against that brief. Rewrite and I re-cut the brief first — type what is wrong in the box beside it, even three words. Drop and the card stays on its procedural glyph, which is a real answer: a glyph that reads is better than an illustration that does not.

The two lines that matter are actor and acting on. Every accepted illustration so far has both; every rejected batch had an actor and nothing to act on. If a row names a thing but no relationship, that row is going to fail, and it is a hundred times cheaper to say so here than after six generations of it.
