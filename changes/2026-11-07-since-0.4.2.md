- **Fixed** — in the Shipyard, clicking your ship, or moving parts once it is open, no longer
  freezes the game for seconds. (#34)
- **Fixed** — in co-op, two ships that start different fights in the same system at the same moment
  now end up in one fight, the one that started first, on both screens. The second ship's log says
  "VELA got here first. You join their fight." Before, it could show the wrong enemy, take hits from
  attacks it was never shown, or never be able to finish the fight.
- **Fixed** — in co-op, SAVE & EXIT or ABANDON in a shared fight now takes your ship out of it. Your
  friends no longer wait on you forever, and CONTINUE puts you back in their fight as a new arrival.
  If the fight ended while you were away, CONTINUE does not start it again, and the log says "The fight
  ended while you were away."
- **Fixed** — in co-op, a slow host no longer leaves you fighting your own copy of the enemy. The log
  says "Waiting for the host." until the answer comes. If your join lands in the same moment as the
  last kill, you share the win instead of getting stuck.
- **Changed** — an ambush on arrival where a friend is already fighting now says "VELA is already
  fighting here. You join their fight." instead of announcing a contact that never shows up.
- **Added** — NEW THINGS on the title menu, between SETTINGS and MESSAGE FROM JON. It opens a page
  in the note's case that lists what changed in each build, newest first: the version, the day it
  went out and a few short lines. Changes not out yet are headed "dev" when run from source and
  "NEXT" with the commit in a test build. A small amber square beside NEW THINGS means this build's
  notes are unread, or a line was added since you last opened it, and opening the page clears it. Jon asked for it on Nov 5. `tools/dev/release.sh` now refuses a version whose NEW THINGS entry
  is not on top and dated.

- **Fixed** — on the sector map, when A.S.H. says "Click a world twice and we fly there", she now
  outlines the world nearest your ship instead of the ship. It is a world a click reaches (not one
  behind a sun or under another event's marker), and not one your ship sits on top of when there is
  another. The box goes round with the world. In a system with no such world, or while you are
  drifting under your own thrust (a click doesn't fly you then), it stays on your ship.
- **Added** — an eye beside GOAL: REACH THE CORE shows how strong the Custodian is getting. Until
  your fifteenth jump it sleeps beside the number of jumps left before it starts to grow. Then it
  wakes, and it opens wider and burns redder as the Custodian grows, until it is at full strength
  at jump 33. Point at it to read what it means. The tutorial step under the goal sits a little lower.
- **Changed** — stations are rarer, about half as many a galaxy, and no station sits within a
  light hull's jump of another. Plan your fuel. A ship that does not can run dry between docks.
- **Changed** — every station has all five decks now, at every level. A laboratory reads relics
  at an outpost too. A station that no manufacturer holds still posts work for one, and that desk
  takes the work back. A Hiring Board with nothing to post says so.
- **Changed** — the Custodian grows stronger with every jump after your fifteenth, up to twice its
  strength. Its attacks do not change.
- **Changed** — in co-op the Custodian is the same for the whole party. It grows with the most
  jumps any ship in the party has made, and the eye beside the goal shows that for everyone. A
  friend who joins late sees it as it is. If the lead ship jumps while you fight the Custodian, it
  grows stronger in the middle of the fight.
- **Added** — the suns of a binary now circle each other. A wide pair goes round once in 19 to 25
  minutes, a close pair in 6 or 7. A world that circles one sun goes round with that sun. Worlds
  that circle both suns, and the gas giants, move as before. In a close pair the suns pass in
  front of each other, and the nearer one is drawn over the farther. The autopilot plans its path
  with the suns where they will be, and a ship in orbit of one sun stays with it. LOCAL shows the
  pair where the map left it. A wide pair's map opens centred on the point the suns go round.
  Reduce motion does not slow the suns.
- **Changed** — while the view zooms between LOCAL, the system map and the star chart, the map
  holds still. The suns, the worlds, your ship and your partners' ships stop, and all of them go
  on together when the move ends.
- **Changed** — in any system, the autopilot no longer leaves a sun's orbit in a tight U-turn that
  the map draws as a hook. A few flights ride further round the orbit first.
- **Changed** — each system keeps its own clock, and the save keeps it too. Worlds stay where they
  were when you come back to a system, instead of starting over from the same places on every
  visit. In co-op the party shares one clock, so partners see the suns and worlds in the same
  places.
- **Changed** — in PAINTED, a binary's bright cloud and its teal hot zone are centred on the point
  the suns go round. They used to sit on one sun.
- **Added** — binary systems. About one ordinary system in four is now a binary, a pair of suns
  that circle each other. There are two kinds. In a wide pair the suns are far apart; a world
  near a sun circles that sun, and it is always hot. In a close pair, one binary in three, the suns
  sit near each other and every world circles both, so a close pair can have a temperate world.
  Gas giants circle both suns in either kind. A wide pair's map opens framed on its two suns and
  their own worlds, and an outlined arrow at the edge of the map points to each event that is off
  the picture, with that event's symbol behind it, as tall as the arrow. Your ship and each
  partner off the picture get an outlined arrow too, named beside it: YOU, or the pilot's name. Zoom out to see the whole system; a second right-click comes back. The sector panel lists
  both suns, and FLY HERE works on either one. The star chart marks a binary, and its STAR row says
  BINARY. One of the pair is an orange dwarf, smaller and cooler than the other. Each world takes the
  light of the sun it circles, and in PAINTED the far cloud's edges catch the light of both. A
  world that circles one sun is small. In orbit of one, your ship gets a thin light outline and
  stays in front of it. The map's words for your orbit, the orbit you will drift into and the
  place you select never cross a sun, and your orbit's words stay off the event beacons.
- **Changed** — the autopilot flies a real path through the pull of the suns and the giants. The
  path is worked out before you click, so the dotted line is the flight the ship will fly, burns
  and all. A flight from place to place takes about 11 seconds, where it took about 7. Flying inside
  a system still costs no fuel, and flying with WASD still feels no pull.
- **Changed** — gas giants are heavier, from a tenth to a quarter of their star. A flight that
  passes close to one bends around it. A giant's page shows its MASS.
- **Fixed** — leaving the sector map during a flight no longer drops it. The ship arrives where it
  was going, and a GO still opens its event. Changing the render style or GRAPHICS during a GO no
  longer loses the event and locks the map.
- **Changed** — during a GO you can hover, zoom and drag the map. Clicks that would start another
  flight still wait until you arrive.
- **Fixed** — a hit that destroys your ship no longer lets your feedback destroy the enemy on the
  way back. Against the Custodian this could end a run as a death and a win at once.
- **Changed** — every ordinary enemy has four times the hull it had, ships and creatures alike.
  Fights last more than twice as long, and fewer ships make it to the core. Jeremy found fights
  over too fast to matter. Enemies hit as hard as before. The Hellbender keeps 90 hull, and the
  Custodian starts at 200.
- **Added** — GRAPHICS can pick LOW by itself on a slow graphics chip. The first time the title
  screen is up, the game times how long its galaxy takes to draw. If this computer can't keep
  up, GRAPHICS goes to LOW from the next screen on. Settings then says PICKED FOR THIS COMPUTER
  under the row, and shows the pick at once if it is open. An Intel chip that is not an Arc
  starts on LOW. On Windows the timing can put HIGH back if the chip keeps up. A Mac can't time
  its chip that closely, so an Intel Mac stays on LOW. An Apple chip never starts on it. On
  RADIANT, LOW is tried before RADIANT gives way to SIMPLIFIED. This happens once. Pick HIGH or
  LOW in Settings and the game never changes it again. A HIGH picked in an older build looks the
  same as no pick, so the game may still pick once there.
- **Changed** — the ship on the sector map is your own ship now, not a small arrow. It is drawn
  from the same picture of your hull that LOCAL shows, shrunk to the map's size, so a heavy is
  longer than a light and a new hull shows there after a swap. A dark hull is drawn a little
  brighter so you can find it against the stars. Jeremy asked for it. Partners in your party are
  still green arrows with their names.
- **Changed** — the engine flame on the sector map is blue now, an ion drive, so flying inside a
  system no longer looks like a jump. The planned burns on its path, the burn marker and the
  braking puffs are the same blue. LOCAL and fights keep their own flames.
- **Changed** — the chassis select shows each frame's real hull after its HULL pips, so a light
  Korvan reads "4 · 29 hull". Geno read 4, 6 and 8 pips against 29, 40 and 57 hull as a mismatch.
  The HULL tooltip no longer says a pip is exactly 7 hull. It says about 7, on the refit screen
  and in the cutaway too. Then it does the sum on your own ship: "Your 29 hull rounds to 4 pips."
- **Changed** — UNDOCK is the only way out of a station. While you are docked, LOCAL on the top
  bar is greyed out and says "Docked. Press UNDOCK to leave." The sector map and the star chart
  still open from the berth so you can look around, but FLY HERE, GO and JUMP read DOCKED and
  the flight keys do nothing until you undock. STATION on the top bar, the map's DOCK and LEAVE on
  the party page take you straight back to the berth. Jeremy found it too easy to leave a
  station without meaning to.
- **Changed** — a jump now goes at most two rings deeper than the ring you are in, up from one.
  Jon ruled it on Oct 27: "Two rings now." Jumps outward or along your ring reach as far as
  before. The quickest way to the core is now 7 jumps, where it was 14. A star inside your reach
  ring that is three rings deeper or more still says TOO DEEP. Its fuel row now reads TWO RINGS IN
  AT MOST. The chart's first survey says two rings as well. From the ring two out from the core, the
  core is a jump like any other when it is in reach. Every galaxy still has a way in.
- **Added** — two test settings for the balance simulator, for the combat study. One makes every
  enemy but the Custodian tougher, and one makes them hit harder. The game itself plays the same.
- **Changed** — the star chart dims a star inside your reach ring that is too deep to jump to.
  It is drawn in a dark, flat gray, its lights too, so it no longer looks like a place you can go.
  It stays gray when you point at it or pick it. Its readout still ends TOO DEEP, and so does JUMP.
  Geno met these near the top of the map, where the tilted chart puts rings close together.
- **Fixed** — the derelict hauler says what you would be claiming. The choice reads "Claim the
  light frame" (or medium, or heavy), and the frame you claim is that one. It is the same frame
  on every visit and for everyone in a party. Jeremy claimed it blind on a heavy ship, got a
  light frame, and had given up "Strip it for scrap" for it.
- **Added** — SCRAP on a derelict hauler's frame you do not want, where LEAVE IT was, on LOCAL's
  rail and on the system map. It pays what "Strip it for scrap" pays, the same credits and the
  same crate, once. It is still there after you back out of TRANSFER, and after a save and load.
  Claim another hull or buy one at a yard while it is still on offer, and it is scrapped for you.
  The log names the crate and says it is in SECTOR LOOT. Saves from 0.4.2-alpha.1 load; a hull
  already on offer in one keeps LEAVE IT.
- **Changed** — on a Mac with a Retina screen the game window now stops at 2X. A new player
  starts in a 2X window, WINDOW SIZE offers 1X and 2X, and a saved 3X window opens at 2X. At
  3X the game drew more than twice the pixels every frame, which costs a laptop's graphics chip
  a lot. BORDERLESS and FULLSCREEN still fill the screen. Windows and Linux are unchanged.
- **Added** — FRAME CAP has a 30 setting, and Settings has a VSYNC row under it, ON by default.
  A 30 cap gives a slow machine steadier frames and uses less power. VSYNC can only be off with
  a cap: picking OFF with FRAME CAP at NONE sets the cap to 60, and setting the cap back to NONE
  turns VSYNC on again. Both are opt-in. The defaults are unchanged, and older settings files
  load with VSYNC ON and their own cap.
- **Changed** — the frame counter (Settings, DISPLAY, FRAME COUNTER) shows more. Beside the
  frames per second it gives the average frame time and the slowest frame of the last second,
  in milliseconds, so a short hitch shows even when the rate looks fine. It sits in the strip
  under every screen's frame, five pixels lower than before, so it no longer runs through the
  bottom bar's line on LOCAL and in a fight or the frame lines on SHIP and in the yard, and no
  longer covers the foot of the cards in CARDS. SAVE MY LOGS also
  writes the look, the window size and how the screen scales it, the frame cap, vsync, the
  renderer and, on a Mac, the battery state. Nothing else on screen changes, and the game runs
  the same.
- **Changed** — the first star chart of a run opens without the pause it had while its
  stars were worked out. They are now worked out in the background while you pick your
  hull. The sector map's drawing is also set up while the title menu sits idle (never
  while you are moving the mouse or pressing a key), so the first sector map holds still
  for less time on slower machines. Nothing looks different.
- **Changed** — the sector map does less work each frame, and nothing on it looks different.
  In a clear sky it no longer draws seven layers of dust that were black and added nothing. The
  orbit lines and the asteroid belts skip what is off the screen. The dotted lines ahead of your
  ship work out where each world will be once for both lines, not four or more times over. A
  system you come back to keeps the colours it had, so the map opens without working them out
  again.
- **Changed** — the big world you orbit on LOCAL takes far less graphics work to draw. Its
  surface is no longer worked out a second time where the first answer already holds, and it
  is no longer worked out below or beside the screen where nobody can see it. The first change
  makes every other world a little cheaper too, the sector map's included. Nothing looks
  different.
- **Changed** — a fight against three or more ships opens faster. Each enemy's hit marks, shield
  and armour are now built once, at the size the pack draws the ship. Before, they were built at
  full size and then again at half size, and the first build was thrown away. On Jon's Mac that
  saves about half a second to a second as a pack's fight opens. Nothing looks different.
- **Changed** — the window around the game draws less each frame. The black edges are drawn
  only where the picture does not reach, a hidden layer that covered the whole window is no
  longer drawn, and the screen filter writes its pixels without reading the window first.
  Nothing looks different. It helps most in a big window. On Jon's Mac the window alone went
  from about 7.8 ms a frame to 5.6 ms at 3X, and from 3.5 ms to 2.6 ms at 2X.
